# Barnacle "Barnaby" Shellsworth

A crustacean of two very different callings.

## The Flute Player

By day, Barnaby is a devoted player of the **piccolo flute** (a "picolo," as he insists, because he's never quite forgiven the instrument for being so small). Perched on a driftwood stage at the edge of the kelp forest, he performs every evening at low tide, his tiny shell doubling as a natural resonator that gives his notes an unmistakable, slightly salty shimmer. Critics call his style "whimsical"; his neighbors call it "why won't he stop."

## The Night Shift

When the moon rises, Barnaby swaps his stage for a **taxi cab** — a converted clamshell with a little yellow "TAXI" sign bolted to the top. He's known for two things: an uncanny ability to find shortcuts through the reef, and an equally uncanny inability to stop giving passengers his unsolicited life advice.

> "You know, I once got lost in a storm for three days. Taught me a thing or two about patience."

## What Makes Barnaby Tick

Barnaby believes that **music and motion are the same thing** — both carry you somewhere. A melody takes you through a feeling; a taxi takes you through a neighborhood. So he does both, back to back, and calls it a living. His passengers are a loyal mix of tired starfish, late-night seahorse students, and the occasional tourist who just wants to see the sunset from the top of the reef.

> "Look, you can't get anywhere without a little rhythm. And you can't make a good rhythm without a little road."
